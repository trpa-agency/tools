The basics

About BMPs

Best Management Practices minimize soil erosion and capture polluted water before it reaches Lake Tahoe.

What is the problem?

Lake Tahoe's clarity

Lake Tahoe's clarity has stabilized after declining at an alarming rate of more than one foot per year since measurements began in 1968. The Lake Tahoe Total Maximum Daily Load has shown that fine sediment and nutrients in stormwater runoff from developed areas cause most of the lake's clarity loss. Implementing BMPs plays a major role in continuing to stabilize, and ultimately restore, the lake's clarity. Thousands of properties are already in compliance. Have you done your BMPs?

What is stormwater runoff?

In an undisturbed watershed, most stormwater is captured by vegetation and absorbed and filtered through the soil. Development, such as roads, driveways, and rooftops, creates impervious surfaces that prevent stormwater from infiltrating. Instead, it runs across the surface, collecting sediment, nutrients, oil, and grease as it travels, enters the nearest storm drain or stream, and ends up in Lake Tahoe.

Many people assume storm drains lead to a sanitary sewer where runoff is treated. At Lake Tahoe that is not true. All storm drains eventually lead to the lake. Runoff carrying fine sediment and nutrients clouds the water and feeds algae, which together have cost the lake more than 30 feet of clarity since the 1960s.

What are BMPs?

BMPs are measures taken to minimize soil erosion and capture polluted water before it enters Lake Tahoe. They improve water quality and slow the decline of the lake's clarity. By pairing BMPs with fire defensible space practices, property owners protect the lake and reduce the threat of catastrophic wildfire at the same time.

Most BMPs fall into three families:

  • Source control keeps soil in place: paved driveways, parking barriers, slope stabilization, armored roof driplines, and revegetation.
  • Collection and conveyance moves runoff from hard surfaces to an infiltration system: swales, slotted channel drains, gutters, and downspout armor.
  • Infiltration soaks runoff into the ground: rain gardens, infiltration trenches and basins, and subsurface systems.

BMPs most often prescribed for homes

A residential BMP evaluation usually comes back with some combination of these.

Pave dirt driveways, parking areas, and roads

Compacted dirt areas routinely disturbed by vehicles are notorious sediment sources, especially during winter snow removal. Compacted dirt areas must be paved and the runoff captured and infiltrated, or restored and blocked off to prevent future disturbance.

Install parking barriers

Parking barriers prevent future soil disturbance from vehicle traffic. They can be large boulders, bollards, a fence, or large established vegetation.

Convey runoff to an infiltration system

Stormwater runoff from impervious surfaces must be captured and conveyed to infiltration systems. Driveway conveyance systems must have an accessible sediment trap ahead of the infiltration system so it can be maintained.

Infiltrate runoff from impervious surfaces

Runoff from roofs, driveways, and walkways must be captured and infiltrated into the soil before it can leave the property. Gravel or rock infiltration systems and infiltration basins are the most common choices.

Stabilize or retain soil

Rock, wood, and vegetation stabilize soils and keep sediment from being carried off site.

Keep defensible space

To be fire defensible space compliant, no combustible materials, such as pine needles, wood chips, or woody vegetation, are permitted within 5 feet of any structure. Gravel and rock, or irrigated herbaceous vegetation, are acceptable alternatives. Visit Tahoe Living with Fire for more.

Vegetation and mulch stabilize soils in the 5 to 30 foot zone. Keep defensible space by spacing vegetation and applying organic mulch, such as wood chips or pine needles, in discontinuous patches.
