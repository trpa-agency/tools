Chapter 60 of the TRPA Code of Ordinances

BMP Requirements

Every developed parcel in the Tahoe Region must install and maintain Best Management Practices. Here is why, and what that means on the ground.

Why install BMPs?

Lake Tahoe's clarity declined at more than one foot per year from the first measurements in 1968 until it stabilized in recent years. The Lake Tahoe Total Maximum Daily Load showed that fine sediment and nutrients in stormwater runoff from developed areas cause most of the loss. BMPs on private property are how that runoff gets caught before it reaches the lake, and they are the private share of the Environmental Improvement Program. Thousands of properties are already in compliance.

Where the runoff goes

In an undisturbed watershed, vegetation and soil catch and filter most stormwater. Roads, driveways, and rooftops change that: runoff races across hard surfaces, picks up sediment, nutrients, oil, and grease, and flows into the nearest storm drain or stream.

Many people assume storm drains lead to a sewer where runoff is treated. At Lake Tahoe that is not true. Every storm drain eventually leads to the lake. Runoff carrying fine sediment and nutrients clouds the water and feeds algae, which together cost the lake more than 30 feet of clarity since the 1960s.

What BMPs are

Best Management Practices are measures that keep soil in place and capture polluted runoff before it enters Lake Tahoe. They fall into three families, and most properties use all three:

  • Source control keeps soil from moving: paved driveways, parking barriers, slope stabilization, armored roof driplines, and revegetation.
  • Collection and conveyance moves runoff from hard surfaces to an infiltration system: swales, slotted channel drains, gutters, and downspout armor.
  • Infiltration soaks runoff into the ground: rain gardens, infiltration trenches and basins, and subsurface systems.

See each family in the photo gallery. Every practice has a fact sheet and a standard drawing in the BMP Toolkit.

The requirements

Chapter 60 (Water Quality) of the TRPA Code of Ordinances requires BMPs on every developed parcel, from a single home to a shopping center. The specifics depend on the property, but these are the pieces a BMP plan has to address. A property that meets them receives a BMP Certificate of Completion.

Property type

Single-family homes usually qualify for the free online Small BMP Retrofit Plan. Commercial, multi-family, tourist, and public properties generate more runoff and pollutants and go through the BMP Retrofit Permit, often with engineered plans. Properties that already hold a certificate stay compliant by maintaining their BMPs.

Source control

Stabilize every sediment source. Bare soil is revegetated or covered with rock or mulch, slopes are held with vegetation, riprap, or walls, compacted dirt driveways and parking areas are paved or restored and blocked with parking barriers, and roof driplines are armored with rock. Toolkit section 4.2.

Defensible space

BMPs are designed to work with fire defensible space. Within 5 feet of any structure, use rock, gravel, or irrigated herbaceous plants, never pine needles, wood chips, or woody shrubs. From 5 to 30 feet, mulch in discontinuous patches and spaced vegetation stabilize soil without carrying fire. See Tahoe Living with Fire.

Infiltration of the 20-year, one-hour storm

Runoff from roofs, driveways, walkways, and other impervious surfaces must be captured and infiltrated on the property. Systems are sized for the 20-year, one-hour storm, about one inch of rain in an hour, using the BMP Calculation Spreadsheet, which also sets the gravel and pipe volumes. Toolkit section 4.1.

Pretreatment

Runoff that carries sediment, such as driveway drainage, passes through an accessible sediment trap or similar device before it reaches the infiltration system, so the system can be cleaned instead of replaced. Toolkit section 4.4.

Site constraints, treat and release

High seasonal groundwater, shallow bedrock, slow-draining soils, or utilities can make full infiltration impossible. A site-constrained property installs source control BMPs, treats runoff to the extent practical, and releases it; it receives a Source Control Certificate. Contact the BMP team to determine whether a property is site constrained. See the site constraint definitions.

Area-wide treatment

In some neighborhoods a public water quality project treats runoff for the whole drainage area. Parcels inside one may meet the infiltration requirement through the project while still doing their source control BMPs. Active and planned projects are drawn on the BMP map, and the EIP Project Tracker has each project's documents.

Lakefront properties

Where runoff cannot be infiltrated before it reaches the shore, a vegetated filter strip or a flow-through path slows and filters it on the way. Chapter 8 of the handbook covers shorezone structures.

Fertilizer

Tahoe soils already contain plenty of phosphorus. Use zero-phosphorus products, apply them in May and September, and water them in gently. Fertilizer is restricted in stream environment zones. Fertilizer at Lake Tahoe.

Temporary BMPs and winterization

Construction sites need fencing, fiber rolls, silt fence, stockpile covers, and drain inlet protection while the ground is open (toolkit section 4.5). Grading is not permitted from Oct. 15 to May 1, and every active site must be winterized by Oct. 15 under the Site Winterization Guidelines.

BMPs most often prescribed for homes

A residential BMP evaluation usually comes back with some combination of these permanent BMPs. Temporary BMPs, the fencing and fiber rolls used while a site is under construction, are a separate set in toolkit section 4.5.

Pave dirt driveways, parking areas, and roads

Compacted dirt areas routinely disturbed by vehicles are notorious sediment sources, especially during winter snow removal. Compacted dirt areas must be paved and the runoff captured and infiltrated, or restored and blocked off to prevent future disturbance.

Install parking barriers

Parking barriers prevent future soil disturbance from vehicle traffic. They can be large boulders, bollards, a fence, or large established vegetation.

Convey runoff to an infiltration system

Stormwater runoff from impervious surfaces must be captured and conveyed to infiltration systems. Driveway conveyance systems must have an accessible sediment trap ahead of the infiltration system so it can be maintained.

Infiltrate runoff from impervious surfaces

Runoff from roofs, driveways, and walkways must be captured and infiltrated into the soil before it can leave the property. Gravel or rock infiltration systems and infiltration basins are the most common choices.

Stabilize or retain soil

Rock, wood, and vegetation stabilize soils and keep sediment from being carried off site.

Keep defensible space

To be fire defensible space compliant, no combustible materials, such as pine needles, wood chips, or woody vegetation, are permitted within 5 feet of any structure. Gravel and rock, or irrigated herbaceous vegetation, are acceptable alternatives.

Vegetation and mulch stabilize soils in the 5 to 30 foot zone. Keep defensible space by spacing vegetation and applying organic mulch, such as wood chips or pine needles, in discontinuous patches.
