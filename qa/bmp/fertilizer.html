Lawns and gardens

Say no to phosphorus fertilizer

Lake Tahoe soils already contain plenty of phosphorus. The excess in fertilizer runs off your property, ends up in the lake, and feeds algae.

Tips for fertilizer application

What type of fertilizer protects the lake?

  • Organic compost, mixed 6 to 12 inches deep.
  • Chemical fertilizer with no phosphorus. The label shows a nutrient ratio, N-P-K. The middle number is P, for phosphorus. Make sure it is 0. Slow release is best for perennials, shrubs, and trees.

When should fertilizer be applied?

  • Spring, after the snowpack has melted, in May.
  • Late summer, in September.
  • Check the forecast first and make sure there is no precipitation coming for a few days.

How much should I use?

  • Multiply the length by the width of the lawn or garden to get the square footage.
  • Turf needs one-half to three-quarters of a pound of nitrogen for every 1,000 square feet.

What do I do after applying it?

  • Water so it soaks 6 to 8 inches into the ground.
  • To avoid washing fertilizer toward the lake, water in short cycles and let it soak in: apply water for 10 to 15 minutes, turn it off for an hour or two, then repeat.

Watering tip: if you see runoff or pooling water, turn the water off and let the soil absorb what is there.
