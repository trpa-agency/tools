BMP Retrofit Program

Get a BMP Certificate

All developed parcels in the Tahoe Region must meet TRPA's BMP requirements. Here is what a certificate is and how to earn one.

What is a BMP Certificate?

Pollutants in stormwater runoff from urban areas threaten Lake Tahoe's clarity. BMPs mitigate the impacts of development by capturing stormwater from impervious surfaces, treating it for pollutants, and mimicking pre-development conditions by infiltrating it into the ground. BMPs also stabilize soil so it cannot migrate off site and reach the lake, and they are consistent with fire defensible space requirements.

Properties in compliance receive a BMP Certificate of Completion, which remains active as long as the BMPs are maintained and functioning properly.

Source Control Certificates

Some sites cannot infiltrate the 20-year, one-hour storm on site because of high seasonal groundwater, shallow bedrock, slow-draining soils, utilities, or other site characteristics. Those property owners implement sediment source control BMPs to reach substantial compliance and receive a BMP Source Control Certificate, until they can participate in an area-wide water quality treatment project.

TRPA tracks compliance in the BMP Database. Use the Property BMP Status Search to check a property's current status, or open the parcel in the LT Info Parcel Tracker to print a copy of its certificate.

The BMP retrofit process

If your property does not have a BMP Certificate, three steps bring it into compliance.

Step 1: Free BMP plan or permit

Small BMP Retrofit Plan. Projects that disturb less than 10 cubic yards of soil can submit a plan electronically and get an approval online. Single-family residential properties typically use this route. You may also pave up to 400 square feet of existing compacted dirt driveway under a Small BMP Retrofit Plan.

How to submit a Small BMP Retrofit Plan

BMP Retrofit Permit. Projects that disturb more than 10 cubic yards of soil, pave more than 400 square feet for the first time, or need engineered plans submit a BMP Retrofit Permit application. Most commercial and multi-family properties require a permit.

BMP Retrofit Permit application (PDF)

Engineers who have attended the BMP Contractors Workshop are on the BMP Installation Service Providers List.

Step 2: Install the BMPs

Once your property has an approved plan or a BMP Retrofit Permit, install the BMPs yourself or hire a qualified contractor. The BMP Toolkit has a fact sheet and standard drawing for every practice, and the videos walk through the most common residential installations.

BMP installation guidance and resources

Step 3: Final inspection

When the BMPs on your Small BMP Retrofit Plan or BMP Retrofit Permit are installed, contact TRPA for a final inspection. If the BMPs were installed as part of a development permit, contact TRPA or the local jurisdiction that issued the permit to schedule the inspection. When the property passes, TRPA issues the BMP Certificate. Properties with certificates stay compliant as long as the BMPs are maintained and functioning.
