TRPA Stormwater Management Program

Keep Tahoe clear, starting at your property line.

Best Management Practices (BMPs) capture stormwater and soak it into the ground before it carries sediment into Lake Tahoe. Every developed parcel in the Tahoe Region is required to install and maintain them.

How it works

Three steps to a BMP Certificate

Thousands of Tahoe properties already have their BMPs in place. If yours does not, this is the path. Most single-family homes finish with a free plan review and no permit.

1

Plan

Submit a free Small BMP Retrofit Plan online for projects that move less than 10 cubic yards of soil. Larger or engineered projects, and most commercial and multi-family properties, apply for a BMP Retrofit Permit.

Which one do I need?

2

Install

Build the BMPs on your approved plan yourself or hire a contractor who has attended the BMP workshop. Standard drawings, sizing worksheets, and videos show exactly what to build.

3

Inspect

Call the BMP Hotline to schedule a final inspection. When the property passes, TRPA issues a BMP Certificate of Completion. It stays active as long as the BMPs are maintained.

Why it matters

Every storm drain leads to the lake

In an undisturbed watershed, most stormwater is caught by vegetation and filtered through the soil. Roads, driveways, and rooftops change that. Runoff races across hard surfaces, picks up fine sediment and nutrients, and flows into the nearest storm drain or stream. At Lake Tahoe, storm drains are not treated. They end in the lake.

The Lake Tahoe Total Maximum Daily Load found that fine sediment and nutrients in runoff from developed areas cause most of the lake's clarity loss. After losing more than 30 feet of clarity since the 1960s, the lake has stabilized. BMPs on private property are the private share of the Environmental Improvement Program and a major part of keeping that trend going.
