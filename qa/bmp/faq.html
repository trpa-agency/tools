Questions and Answers

The questions the BMP Hotline hears most. If yours is not here, call (775) 589-5202 or email bmp@trpa.gov.

Common questions

How do I get a BMP Certificate?

Three steps: a free plan or permit, installation, and a free final inspection. Most single-family homes use the online Small BMP Retrofit Plan; commercial and multi-family properties use the BMP Retrofit Permit. Get a BMP Certificate walks through it.

How do I find out whether a property has a certificate, or print a copy?

Use the Property BMP Status Search. Type a parcel number, an address, an owner name, or a certificate number. Select the parcel and choose "Print certificate on LT Info"; the Lake Tahoe Info Parcel Tracker prints the certificate on file.

What are BMPs, and what does TRPA require?

Best Management Practices keep soil in place and soak stormwater into the ground before it reaches the lake. Chapter 60 of the TRPA Code of Ordinances requires them on every developed parcel. BMP Requirements explains each piece: source control, defensible space, infiltration of the 20-year one-hour storm, pretreatment, site constraints, area-wide treatment, lakefront, fertilizer, and temporary BMPs.

Are BMPs required when a property is sold?

Disclosure is required; installation at sale is not. The seller discloses the property's BMP status on TRPA's form before the sale, and the buyer submits the form to TRPA within 30 days. The disclosure form and what to do with it.

What happens if I do not do my BMPs?

Every property without BMPs is out of compliance with the Code. TRPA focuses enforcement where it matters most for the lake: by land use and pollutant load, closeness to the lake or a stream environment zone, complaints, and nearness to public water quality projects. If you receive a Notice of Failure to Implement Water Quality Improvements by certified mail, contact the TRPA Environmental Specialist named in the letter to work out next steps.

I cannot afford BMPs right now. What should I do?

Call the BMP Hotline. BMPs can be phased over time so the cost comes in manageable pieces, many of the common residential BMPs are do-it-yourself, and staff can identify the least costly options for your property and an approved schedule. The plan review, the permit, and the inspection are free.

Is there a deadline?

The deadlines have passed. The last priority watershed deadline was Oct. 15, 2008, so any developed property without a certificate is already out of compliance. The practical answer is: start now.

Who can install BMPs?

You can, or a contractor can. The installation resources include standard drawings, a materials list, the installation videos, and the list of service providers who have attended TRPA's BMP Installation Training.

How do BMPs fit with fire defensible space?

They are designed together. Within 5 feet of a structure use rock, gravel, or irrigated herbaceous plants, not pine needles, wood chips, or woody shrubs; from 5 to 30 feet, mulch in discontinuous patches and spaced plants hold soil without carrying fire. Tahoe Living with Fire covers the fire side.

Do I really have to pave my dirt driveway?

Compacted dirt that vehicles drive on, especially during winter snow removal, is one of the biggest sediment sources in the Region. The dirt area must be paved with its runoff captured and infiltrated, or restored and blocked off with parking barriers so it is no longer driven on.

What is a site constraint, and how do I know whether my property has one?

High seasonal groundwater, shallow bedrock, slow-draining soils, or utilities can make it impossible to infiltrate the full 20-year one-hour storm on site. A site-constrained property installs source control BMPs and receives a Source Control Certificate. Contact the BMP team to determine whether a property is site constrained; the definitions describe each case.

My certificate is years old. Is it still good?

Yes, as long as the BMPs are maintained and working. Commercial and multi-family owners document their maintenance and TRPA reissues the certificate with a new date. Maintenance explains what to keep and what to send.
